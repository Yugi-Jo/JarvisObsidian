Goal: buy a first home. Joel is currently on UC + PIP and living with his mum, working a
freelance retainer for one client who may move him to a salary.

- No housing element in the UC award (lives with his mum) → **higher work allowance £710/mo**.
- £500/mo = £6,000/yr, which is below the NI Lower Earnings Limit (£6,708) — no qualifying

- £500/mo is ~9 hrs/week at the £12.71 NLW — fine as a retainer, not a job.

## The UC maths

- Taper: 55p per £1 of net earnings above the work allowance. **There is no salary that
  leaves him worse off in cash** — more is always better.
- Nil point = work allowance + max UC award ÷ 0.55 = 710 + 854.70 ÷ 0.55 = **£2,264/mo
  take-home ≈ £32,845 gross**. That is the salary at which UC runs out entirely.
- First £710/mo of take-home is completely taper-free.

## Mortgage — what he could borrow now

Counted income depends entirely on the lender. PIP £8,148/yr; UC standard allowance only
£5,099/yr; full UC £10,256/yr; retainer £6,000/yr.

| Lender style | Counted income | 4× | 4.5× |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PIP + full UC + earned | £24,405 | £97,619 | £109,822 |
| PIP + UC standard allowance only + earned | £19,247 | £76,989 | £86,612 |
| Same, but earned becomes PAYE £18k | £31,247 | £124,989 | £140,612 |
| Same, PAYE £18k + full UC | £36,405 | £145,619 | £163,822 |

- **PIP is the most widely accepted benefit** — many lenders at 100%.
- **UC is usually standard allowance only.** Principality explicitly excludes the health
  (LCWRA) element — so his £854.70 counts as ~£425 there. Nottingham accepts UC at 100%
  excluding housing.
- Most lenders that count benefits still want **earned income alongside**.
- **PAYE vs self-employed is the big lever.** Self-employed = 2–3 years of declared income
  and SA302s, and a single-client retainer reads as fragile. PAYE = 3 months of payslips.
  Moving the £500 retainer to £18k PAYE adds roughly £50–55k of borrowing.

## Deposit

- 5% is the practical floor. Fixed £5–10k first-time-buyer products exist (Lloyds £5k,
  Santander £10k, Newcastle/YBS £5k). Government Mortgage Guarantee Scheme covers 5% up
  to £600k.
- Add ~£2,000–3,000 for legal fees, survey and arrangement → **target ~£12,000 all-in**.
- Any gift from his mum must be documented with a non-repayable letter.

### The catch: savings reduce UC

UC capital rules: below £6,000 ignored; £6,000–£16,000 → tariff income of £4.35/mo per £250
over £6,000; **above £16,000 → no UC at all**.

| Capital | UC cut/mo |
| --- | --- |
| £6,000 | £0 |
| £8,000 | £34.80 |
| £10,000 | £69.60 |
| £12,000 | £104.40 |
| £16,000 | £174.00 (ceiling) |
| £17,000 | UC stops |

So the deposit run has to stay under £16,000 of total capital, and he should expect to lose
up to ~£174/mo of UC while holding it. That is worth it — the alternative is not saving.

### Lifetime ISA

- 25% government bonus, up to £4,000 paid in per year (max £1,000 bonus).
- Must be 18–39 to open; first-time buyer only; property cap £450,000.
- **LISA balance counts as UC capital** — same £6k/£16k rules apply.
- 25% withdrawal charge if not used for a qualifying home (20% if the property is over
  £450k, from 29 Oct 2026).
- A new First Time Buyer ISA replaces the LISA from April 2029 — not relevant on this
  timeline, but no transfers from LISAs will be permitted.

### Chosen route (7 Nov 2026): £6,000 saved + £6,000 gift from his mum = £12,000

- **The gift counts as capital, not income.** One-off gifts from family are not unearned
  income, so it is *not* deducted pound-for-pound — but it does join his capital pot.
  £12,000 total capital → tariff income £104.40/mo → **UC reduced by £104.40/mo**, worth
  ~£1,253 across a year of holding it. Still a clear net win: the gift buys £6,000 of
  deposit for ~£1,250 of UC.
- **Headroom is £4,000.** Total capital must stay under £16,000 or UC stops entirely. At
  £12,000 he can save another £4,000 before that bites — worth knowing, because it means
  the £500/mo retainer should be spent or routed carefully rather than simply banked.
- **The home you live in is disregarded for UC capital**, so once the deposit converts into
  property, the capital problem disappears.
- **Gifted deposit paperwork is real.** The lender/solicitor will want a signed declaration
  from his mum covering: her name, address and relationship; the amount; that it is a
  genuine gift with no repayment, no interest and no stake in the property; that she will
  not live there; and that she is financially solvent. Plus AML checks on *her*: photo ID,
  proof of address, and 3–6 months (sometimes 12) of bank statements showing the money
  building up, with an explanation of where it came from.
- **Practical:** the money should go **directly from her account to the solicitor.** If it
  passes through Joel's account first, the conveyancer has to run source-of-funds checks on
  every account it touched, which drags the purchase out.

### The deposit is no longer the bottleneck

Borrowing is capped by income (~£77k–£110k today), not by savings. £12,000 covers a 5–10%
deposit plus ~£2,750 of fees on a property at the top of what he can currently borrow. So
the constraint to attack is the salary/PAYE conversation, not more saving.

### Local lending and conveyancing traps

- **Coal mining search.** The whole South Wales coalfield. Most lenders expect a CON29M
  search as standard — circa £40–£80 — and recorded workings nearby can trigger a structural
  survey requirement.
- **Valley terraces are solid stone or brick with no cavity**, so damp is common. Commission
  an independent RICS survey (Level 2 or 3) — the lender's valuation confirms the price, not
  the condition.
- **Check construction type.** Some valleys properties are no-fines concrete and are hard or
  impossible to mortgage on standard terms.

## Two things not to bank on

- **Support for Mortgage Interest is a loan, not a grant.** Needs three months on UC, covers
  interest only on up to £200,000, and accrues interest (~4.50% from July 2026) repayable
  when you sell.
- **Do not let UC run to nil accidentally.** A nil award closes the claim after six
  consecutive nil assessment periods, which loses the LCWRA determination — and if he is a
  pre-April-2026 protected claimant, a fresh assessment drops that element from £429.80 to
  £217.26.
