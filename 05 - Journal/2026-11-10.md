# 2026-11-10

## Mobility research — powerchairs, hoists, exoskeletons

Joel is an ambulatory wheelchair user at 400 lb (181 kg) with chronic illness and needs
something he can transport without straining. Four questions, answered with live reads:

- **Folding powerchairs rated for 181 kg.** He is at the ceiling of the whole UK market.
  MobilityPlus+ FortisHD £999.99 (180 kg, 28 kg folded, 51 cm seat), MX-MAX Bariatric £1,399
  (185 kg, 25 kg), KWK D09 XL £2,445 at Sheffield Mobility (180 kg, 21" seat), Pride Jazzy
  Carbon Max HD around £2,500 (182 kg, 17–18 kg with battery, lightest folded lift). The
  Robooter E60 Pro is the only genuine all-terrain folder but caps at 150 kg, so it's out.
- **Getting it in the car.** Every chair at his weight weighs 25–28 kg folded, so a hoist is
  the real answer. Brig-Ayd 40kg Mk2 £1,295 fitted, Autochair Next Gen LM from £1,495,
  Smart Lifter LC/LM from £1,845, Smart Lifter LP from £2,095. MobilityPlus's own portable
  hoist is £349.99 but weighs 12 kg itself and was showing out of stock. Two catches worth
  remembering: a hoist lifts but doesn't fold or clip, and the boot opening has to take a
  66 cm wide folded chair.
- **Funding routes.** VAT relief is 20% off on a self-declaration. Motability leases a
  powered wheelchair or a car with a boot hoist on the enhanced mobility PIP rate, with many
  adaptations free if ordered at the start of the lease. NHS wheelchair services decide
  bariatric powered chairs case by case and won't supply an outdoor-only chair.
- **Exoskeletons and wearables — there's nothing for him.** The whole category tops out
  around 100–130 kg. Clinical devices (ReWalk 100 kg, Indego 113 kg) are £55k–£170k,
  prescription only, need forearm crutches and a trained spotter, and are for spinal injury
  gait. Passive wearable chairs (Archelis 80–115 kg, Noonee Chairless Chair 130 kg) are for
  people who stand for work. Consumer units (Hypershell, Dnsys, about $999–$1,999, 2.5 kg)
  give roughly 30 kg of assist and are hiking aids.
- **Frame materials.** Carbon fibre is the lightest heavy-duty option: CFRP is about
  1.6 g/cm³ with specific strength near 1,000 kN·m/kg, against 187 titanium, 161 magnesium,
  102 aluminium. Magnesium is the lightest metal at 1.74–1.8 g/cm³, around 35% lighter than
  aluminium, but weaker and dearer. In practice carbon buys about 10 kg over aluminium, and
  the frame is a small share of the total — motors, battery, wheels and seat dominate.

## New project: carbon fibre wearable chair

Joel's own design idea, captured as a project note. Trousers that lock into a seating
position, motorised wheels that clip on, wheels and battery in a backpack, forming a
backwards tripod. A market survey read this session found no product that combines powered
assist with a lockable seat, so the gap is real.

One design correction recorded: the battery should ride on the base near the axle, not in a
backpack on the shoulders, because a pack heavy enough for useful range puts load through the
spine, which is the worst place for it at his size.
